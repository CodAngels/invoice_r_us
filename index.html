The problem

The hard part starts after the invoice goes overdue.

Every AR system you own is good at telling you an invoice is late. None of them do the work that comes next: figure out why the customer hasn't paid, dig through email and the ledger for context, decide the next action, chase the right person, remember the date they promised, and come back to check.

Invoices don't simply become overdue. They get stuck — behind a missing PO, a wrong contact, a dispute, a forgotten promise, a document nobody sent, or a teammate who never replied.

Missing PO AP won't process the invoice until a purchase-order number is referenced on it.
Invoice not received They want another copy, or it went to a contact who no longer works there.
Promise to pay "It goes out Friday." Somebody has to actually check on Monday.
Dispute Quantity, pricing or whether the work was finished. A person has to resolve it.
Documentation request A W-9, a statement, a supporting document, or a corrected invoice.
Internal dependency Collections can't answer until Sales or Operations weighs in.

The concept

A live case for every unpaid invoice.

Octto opens a Collection Case per invoice, and that case owns the workflow until the balance settles. The ledger and the inbox are inputs. The blocker, the next action, the follow-up and the resolution are what comes out.

INVOICE #18372 ACME Corp $18,420 overdue
Blocker Customer requested that PO 88219 be referenced on the invoice. Read from a reply in the AR inbox on Sep 4.
Next action Update the invoice and resend to AP. Drafted and waiting in your queue — nothing sends on its own.
Follow-up Sep 15, only if the balance is still open and no newer reply has arrived.
State WaitingInternal approval on the corrected invoice.

Illustrative case. Invoice numbers, customers and amounts on this page are examples, not results from a live account.

Why this isn't a reminder tool

Follow-ups expire when they stop being true.

A calendar reminder fires whether or not it still makes sense — which is how customers get chased for invoices they already paid. An Octto follow-up stays alive only while its conditions hold.

FOLLOW UP · SEP 15 · INVOICE #18372
ONLY IFbalance due > $0.00
ANDno newer customer response
ANDinvoice not voided
ANDcase not paused for dispute

If it gets paid, voided, disputed or the customer writes back first, the scheduled action re-evaluates or cancels itself. Nobody on your team has to remember to call it off.

Access & control

It reads your client's books. It does not run them.

You are the one holding the relationship and the fiduciary duty, so the early product earns trust in steps. It does not need autonomous control of financial records to be useful on day one.

System What it gets What it never gets Why
QuickBooks / accounting Read-only No writes Invoices, balances, due dates, customer records and payment state.
Gmail / Outlook Read + draft No sending Understand replies and prepare responses for a person to review.
Invoice modification Recommend only No autonomous edits It proposes the correction; a human makes it, until you say otherwise.
Payments Observe settlement No payment authority Sees the balance clear, then closes the case and kills future follow-ups.

The trust ladder

Autonomy should expand because you ask for it, not because the architecture allows it.

STEP 1 Read See the ledger and the thread.
STEP 2 Recommend Name the blocker and the next action.
STEP 3 Draft Write the reply, leave it unsent.
STEP 4 Human approval Your staff releases every action.
STEP 5 Limited automation Only the low-risk actions you nominate.

Steps 1–3 are where a design partner starts. Steps 4 and 5 are opt-in, per client account.

Why we're starting with firms

You work receivables for a dozen businesses at once.

A single company sees its own aging report. Your firm sees a dozen of them, across different industries, with different customers who get stuck in different ways. That makes you the fastest way to learn what this workflow actually looks like — and the hardest customer to fool.

One case queue per client, kept separate Separate connections, separate books, separate inboxes. Nothing crosses between client accounts.
Your staff stays the approver Drafts land in a queue for a person on your team. Your name is still the one on the email your client's customer receives.
A month-end artifact you can hand to the client Cases closed, cash collected, average resolution time, promises still outstanding — a record of the work, separated from credits, write-offs and duplicates.
Built around the cases that need a human Disputes and internal dependencies get escalated, not chased. The teammate is there to clear the routine ones so your people spend their hours on the rest.

Where this actually stands

This is not a finished product, and we're not going to pretend it is.

Octto is pre-launch. Everything on this page is the workflow we intend to build, described honestly — not a feature list you can log into this afternoon. The numbers above are examples.

We're doing the market investigation before the product hardens around assumptions, because the engineering is not the scarce resource here. Understanding how receivables are genuinely worked today is. That's the whole reason we're calling firms first.
